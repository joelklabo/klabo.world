Evidence rule

The 1924 papers say Brown Court, not the modern street number. Later transfer records and the 2007 retrospective bridge the identity to 28 Brown Court.

Narrative storyline

A new family home used first as a public demonstration.

The house now known as 28 Brown Court was built in 1924 as the new family home of Tully R. Cornick, proprietor of Cornick Electric Company at 110 Kentucky Street. Before the Cornick family moved in, the house was opened to the public for a week as Petaluma’s first Electrical Home / all-electric demonstration house.

The open house ran Sunday, July 13 through Sunday, July 20, 1924. Newspaper articles and advertisements described the house as a showcase of the “completely wired home”: more than forty outlets, room-by-room appliances, electric range, dishwasher, laundry equipment, heaters, lighting controls, radio, and other conveniences meant to show how electricity could change domestic life.

Why it mattered in 1924

Domestic electrification was still a selling point, not a background assumption. The July 12, 1924 Petaluma Argus feature, “Uses of Electricity to Be Demonstrated,” is the anchor source. It places the model electrical home at Brown Court off D Street and lists a room-by-room appliance inventory.

What was inside

Electricity as a room-by-room lifestyle demo.

The Electrical Home was promoted as complete wiring, not just a few fixtures. Its appliance list included a radio, electric heaters, fans, vacuum cleaners, grill, coffee urn, toaster, Hotpoint range, Walker dishwasher, Thor washer and ironer, hair/curling equipment, sewing machines, bathroom heater, and immersion heater.
